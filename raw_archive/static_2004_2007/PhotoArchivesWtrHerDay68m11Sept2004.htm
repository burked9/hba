HERITAGE  BOAT  ASSOCIATION

"Celebrating Ireland's Floating Heritage"

         

 


Once tied up in Dromineer we split up the crew into a gang in the fields filling trailers which were brought down by tractor to the boat where we had another gang loading. We started loading at 10.30 and while we had a quick stop for lunch progress was good. During the day we had continuous stream of visitors taking photos and asking what we were doing including a crew from the local newspaper the Nenagh Guardian. By 14.30 we had the boat full with 1,000 bales of barley straw weighing something like 25 tonnes. After a few photos to record the occasion we adjourned to the Whisky Still where we had some sustenance. The worst of the weather held off during the day even though we had passing showers. The wind which was our biggest concern got stronger though, so we knew we couldnt move out with our cargo behind us until it got calmer. As some of our crew were under pressure to get back home we decided to leave the straw behind us and headed back across the lake to Dromaan. It got calmer later on in the evening and as the following days weather was promised even worse we set off again as it was getting dark we went back and brought our cargo across the lake without any difficulty.

ECONOMICS

In total we brought 1,000 bales of straw which was eight tractor trailer loads. The journey by road is sixty miles round trip. It would have taken four days to do this with one tractor. Each trip would have consumed seven gallons of diesel so instead of fifty six gallons of diesel it took less than three. The only down side of bringing it by boat is the fact that the cargo had to be handled two extra times (on to & off the boat).

Other than that it was a fun day and a great way to spend Water Heritage Day 2004.

Gerry Burke

Skipper 68M & ESB 2.

 Crew: Odran Burke, Colm Burke, Gerry Burke, John White, Owen OLeary, PJ Slattery, Tom Bleach, Michael ORourke, Jody Bleach, Daniel Burke.

Log of 68M, Water Heritage Day, Saturday September 11th 2004

Scheduled departure time was 08.00 but we were 30 minutes late when we headed out of Church Bay across Lough Derg to Dromineer. 68M this journey was towing ESB 2 an Ammunition Barge built in Hamburg and brought to Ireland in 1926 for the building of the Hydro electric scheme in Ardrnacrusha.

The weather for the weekend was promised bad with rain and wind neither of which was conducive to the job in hand but as the task was specifically organised to co inside with Water Heritage Day it wasn't bad enough to put us off. Even though it was mid September the lake level was low for this time of the year so to get out of our moorings we had to put two men aboard the Hamburg to use barge poles if required. Luck was on our side and we had no difficulty getting out of the tricky bay with its numerous submerged under water hazards. Once out on the lake we had to contend with taking the weather on our beam before we could turn North East and head to Dromineer. We entered the harbour the long way around Goose Island so that we were heading up wind into the old quay wall beside the former Grand Canal Company shed.
