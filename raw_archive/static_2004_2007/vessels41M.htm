Builders: Vickers, 1927. L 61Ft 6in W 13Ft 6in D 3Ft 6in. Payload 60 Tonnes. . Draft Now 3Ft 6in. Engine 120HP Perkins 6354. Gearbox PRM 3:1

The 41M was built in Dublin in the first half of 1927. Prior to commissioning for service she was brought down the canal and weighed and calibrated in Killaloe. When weighed the following stores were aboard, 40 planks, 6 covers, 2 poles, 1 boat hook, 3 rope fenders, 1 cork fender, 2 stop ropes, 2 hand ropes, 3 lash lines, 2 trippers, 1 empty cask, 1 anchor and 120 gallons of fuel oil for the Bollinder engine.

Working History

She then went into service with the Grand Canal Company (GCC) carrying general cargo including porter for Guinnesss, manure from the city to the farms and flour and potatoes back into the city.

41M is enshrined in memory on the Plaque of the Tourist Office wall in Carrick-on-Shannon. This plaque commemorates the last commercial trip of 41M before decommissioning, where she carried 20 tons of Flour from Limerick to Carrick-on-Shannon in May 1959. Some interesting accidents fell on the 41M during her lifetme. In one such accident she sank in the lock at Killaloe with a cargo of cement, but was fortunately refloated prior to setting of the cargo. A second interesting accident occurred when during the small hours of the morning 41M noticed an oncoming barge at Edenderry aquaduct, and in attempting to avoid an accident gently hit the supporting wall, with the consequential emptying of the level. The scars are still evident today on the bow section at the waterline. 41M was de-commissioned with the rest of the fleet in 1960, and was later sold off by CIE at auction.

Early History after Auction

41M was purchased at auction and later sold as a swap for an Island in Lough Key. John Conway (ex Emerald Star Carrick-on-Shannon) put a new roof and windows from a scrap factory roof into her. He later sold her for 2500 pounds to clear a gambling debt. At that time, a Gardner 4 cylinder engine was also installed, but was never satisfactory, and was later swapped for a Leyland E170 which was originally built for the Matilda Mark 4 Tanks. Later, the engine was installed as a donkey engine in a trawler operating out of Arklow, before finally settling in the 41M where she served admirably for 15 years. The old Gardner engine was still seen working in the back yard of Mick Donohoe (lock keeper at Ballycommon) for many years later and been used to chop up logs, whereas the Leyland E170 is buried in Gortmore Boat Club. Tim Keating bought her c.1975 and added rails and a new floor. However, Tim had lost interest by 1978 and she was brought to Tullamore, tied up and forgotton for 6 years.

New lease of life

In 1984, while travelling lazily down to Shannon Harbour from Lowtown with Brian Hadfield, we stopped in Tullamore and I saw the 41M lying very sad against the wall. I tried for three months to locate the owner, and eventually the 41M became my property. Having being underwater for 6 years we managed to start the engine (Thanks to Noel Lynam and Albert black), which served well for the next 15 years. During this period, I have renovated and added, so that now the 41M has a new (well sort of new)Perkins 6354 engine (ex Combined Harvester) with a 3 to 1 Hydraulic PRM box driving the original Cast Iron Prop. I also renewed the roof, replaced the windows with modern Double glazed PVC, added a wheelhouse, and replated her entire bottom. I also added new rails on the stern, and poured cement into the ballast tank. The interior has been completely redesigned with cabins, shower room, full size fitted kichen, kerosene stove, and carpets throughout, A true home from home. This year we added new internal ceilings throughout , and replaced the cuddy roof in the forward section, raising it by 9 inches and adding double glazed windows. In summer 2003 we joined the Heritage Boat Association trip to the Erne Systems and had the distinction of being the centre barge in the raft of 16 heading into Enniskillen, from which the raft was directed and filmed for UTV (Ulster Television). The 41M now cruises the Shannon in comfort and style with great reliability, and is set to continue to do so for many more years.

Whats Living Heritage?
