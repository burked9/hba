M, B, or E = Motor, Bye Trader or Engineering Boat          Back to Vessels

Canal Barges were an integral part of the Irish transportation system from the late 1800's to the 1960's. Canal boats as they were known started with the horse pulled barges which were given letters and a number.   In the early 1900's a new barge was introduced with single cylinder Bolinder engines and had no reverse.  These Barges were called "M" (for Motor) boats and various companies made them, there were slight differences between the various companies but in general the boats were all the same. Most of the "M" boats worked day and night, the Grand Canal Company owned these and their numbers went from 31M to 79M. The private men were called hackers or private companies operating a canal cargo trade service were called bye-traders, there were about forty of these boats; their numbers ended with a "B".  Canal navigation required constant management by the canal companies and boats charged with or used for engineering were given the suffix "E".  Some of those boats were custom built for maintenance works but more often than not were older "M" or "B" boats that had come to end or their cargo carrying life. If still in good enough order these were commandeered for engineering procedures. See the story of 4E, a canal boat that  has had all four identities of horse, motor, bye-trader and engineering boat.
