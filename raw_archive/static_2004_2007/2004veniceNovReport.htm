HBA Invades Venice 19th - 22nd November 2004

Not since Napoleans galleon appeared in the lagoon exactly 200 years ago has Venice been faced with an invasion force to match the sixteen souls from the HBA who threatened her in November. Latest reports indicate that Venice is recovering well from this invasion conducted under the stewardship of "skipper" Cormac and recently promoted "admiral" Lorna Baker Kenny. Whether the HBA crew will ever make a full recovery only time will tell!

A wonderful trip grew out of a casual exchange initiated by Cormac Kenny in Portumna harbour one balmy Saturday afternoon such a short time before. "Im thinking of organising a trip to Venice in November, would you be interested?" Well we were and he did. And what a trip it turned out to be. Accommodation was organized, plane tickets were booked and all we had to do was turn up and enjoy ourselves. On a trip to Venice the second part of that is especially easy as one of our group described Venice  "a theme park for boaties".

After a slight delay taking off but with time made up in transit we arrived on a crisp November Friday evening almost on schedule in Marco Polo airport (apparently named after some guy who invented a peppermint sweet). Our intrepid guide, Cormac, engaged in brief negotiations with local entrepreneur and in a flash we were ensconced in two water taxis to take us across the lagoon directly to the steps of our hotel, Locanda La Corte near Giovanni e Paolo. That trip in itself with the brio of the taxi engines and the character of the boats was a great introduction to the adventure that was to be our visit to Venice.

Gleesons and The Ballroom of Romance

We arrived at our hotel in time for a quick turnaround and in time for aperitifs in Ostaria al Ponte a great little bar near our hotel run by Ivan and Romeo (yes thats his real name!). Straight away it was clear the benefits of having our two "local" guides Cormac and Lorna (or is that Lorna and Cormac?). You will often be warned what an expensive place Venice can be but our Treasurer for the weekend Joe Treacy, the tours recipient of the coveted Britvic Award, confirmed that a round for our intrepid group was working out at a modest 35 a go. This hostelry was to become our local over the weekend largely due to the warm welcome and hospitality we received every time from our innkeepers and for those with canal connections it became the Gleesons of Venice.

Off we headed after this to Osteria Alberto up the street for a prebooked dinner. What the locals made of this crowd of Paddys who, Ballroom of Romance style, occupied two tables with the women at one and the men at another will never be known due to the language barrier. Mind you one of the males did get in touch with his romantic side by buying up half the roses that were for sale in Venice that evening. It was never discovered what was their ultimate fate.

After dinner the group wandered in search of further hospitality in a city that mostly closes a bit earlier than what we might be accustomed to on the Shannon. Having wandered in ever diminishing circles for a while and pretending not to be lost we met up with Gabriella, a friendly waitress from our restaurant on her way home. Im sure she was amazed we were still up at our age but she pointed us in the direction of a later opening hostelry where we were served by a barmaid from New Zealand whose parents were from Kilkenny. A nice wind down to the evening included a spontaneous rendition of "From Clare to Here"  with some of us wondering if it really was as the song goes "a long long way" given the amount of wandering we had done.

Time for home and we headed in tow of Cormac via a Piazza San Marco devoid of other tourist for some reason  could it be anything to do with the hour of what had now become morning?

X Marks the Spot

Breakfast the next morning and the crew assembled in stages  most of them that is but not a rose in sight!  no sign of Mick or May either!

Saturday was a day for touring the city in smaller groups at our leisure with an optional rendevous at a restaurant near Ponte di Scalzi marked with an X on our maps by Cormac. Some of us decided to go there by "vaparetto" the local waterbus system. We hadnt planned to go via the island of Murano but inadvertently did given we got on a stop too early! This turned out to be a great way to get a good introduction to the city. It was fascinating to see how the waterway, to us a tourist attraction, was a real living navigation with all commuting and goods transport within Venice done by water. We enjoyed the highly efficient waterbus system as it passed all other manner of water transport ferrying to and from businesses and markets. We got on the number 1 route and traveled up the Grand Canal past all the wonderful sights on one of the finest "main streets" in the world finally arriving at our destination.

Off we went in search of our restaurant which must have heard we were coming because it had closed down! Undaunted we picked out a local alternative and settled in later to be joined by most of the rest of the group. In this part of Venice we were just a stones throw from the depots where all the land based transport of trains and trucks distribute their cargo for transfer to the Venetian water based system. You would have to be impressed with the efficiency of all the water based transport systems. Apart from their tourist attraction they really work!

A nice lunch followed by a bit more sight seeing for some and shopping for others then back to the hotel to prepare for dinner. A quick aperitif in our local with Ivan and Romeo, old friends by now, and we are off again

Led by our skipper Cormac (admiral Lorna is a great delegator) we headed off on the now familiar autobus system to our restaurant near Accademia. Well fed we headed back towards our hotel. On the autobus on the way back Venice was treated to what is believed to be the first ever rain dance performed by Pocahontas on a "vaparetto" in that famous city. Entertaining though it was it was happily ineffective as the weather held up for the rest of the weekend. Off the waterbus and home via the now inevitable detour to a deserted Saint Marks square. We never did figure out if Cormacs detours were deliberate or if he was just lost again.

 

Torcello and Burano

Our intrepid scout had offered the option of Sunday lunch at Locanda Cipriani on the island of Torcello about an hours boat trip from Venice itself. Most of the group opted for this and it turned out to be a highlight. A beautiful day, a wonderful boat trip out and a magnificent lunch on arrival served by our fast talking waiter, Marino, in whom the HBA comedians had more than met their match. Was he joking or serious when he said we drank enough wine to run a Ferrari? How quick he was when Robbie Bayly asked for a mug for his tea to remark that he already had enough mugs with him from Ireland! For anybody visiting Venice Torcello really deserves to be on the itinerary time permitting. If your waiters name is Marino dont try and outquip him  hell win.

We set off home on Sunday evening into a beautiful red sky taking a pit stop, just to help the local economy, on Burano island on the way home. Back to Venice for a quick turnaround (God, Im feeling my age!) and out for our last evening in Venice. For some at this time a visit the church Santa Maria della Pieta made famous by the music of Vivaldi is a must. But like our Saturday restaurant it was closed  did someone tell them we were coming? Then across the temporary pontoon bridge and a visit to the Church of Santa Maria della Salute built to celebrate the end of the euphemistically called "plague" in Venice. They sure knew how to enjoy themselves them there Venetians!

Little subgroups visited different places in the city and eventually like homing pigeons we ended up in the one trattoria near our hotel for a supper of real Italian pizza and, of course, some more Ferrari fuel. A short visit to the local Irish Pub en route back to the hotel and for some an early night, for others the hospitality of their suite from Linda and Jimmy (next years tour Commodore?) and cocktails before finally (eventually!) retiring for the night.

"Deoch an Dorais" at Gleesons

Monday breakfast and all of us, well almost all, still firing on all plugs in spite of a hectic schedule. Our flight time of 7 pm gave us the opportunity for a last look around Venice. For some even more shopping  yes, its true! For others a last walk around the city or a last trip on the Grand Canal or a visit to the Maritime Musuem.

Our last "appointment" in Venice was a final visit to our "local" Ostaria al Ponte for a final "deoch" with Ivan and Romeo. To thank them for their hospitality they were presented with one of our crew caps which had been organised by Gerry Burke to commemorate the trip. Joe was delighted to note that neither Ivan nor Romeo thought the cap was "lousy"! The cap now stands on display in the Ostaria as a proud record of the HBAs first overseas trip. A quick farewell drink and back again to the airport by water taxi direct from our hotel  by general agreement the way to travel in style. There is no truth in the rumour that the Italian news later carried a story about the generous tipping of Irish tourists featuring an interview with a delirious taxi driver.

And on schedule, a flight back to Dublin ended what had been a wonderful trip. A hard act to follow but there is already talk of a repeat next year to another destination and a Commodore has been identified, but I promised Jimmy I wouldnt mention his name. A special word of thanks for this trip to the skipper and admiral for making the suggestion in the first place and for putting the advance thought into the weekend.
