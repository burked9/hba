History Of Canal Boat 34 B                    Back to Vessels

34B near Bolands Lock, Grand Canal, June 2004.


Canal Boat 34 B was built in Shannon Harbour in 1896 .In her earlier years she is described as "The Icebreaker ". It would appear she was used for this purpose as she has a distinctive curved bow underwater, which would have facilitated this activity. She was owned by Shackeltons in 1911 and two years later she was sold to Moores of Graiguecullen (Carlow) when the mills owned by Shackeltons at Milford went into liquidation. She was used by the Moore family to carry general cargo on the Barrow and Grand Canal until trade declined on the system. In the 30's & 40's she was used to transport turf from Ticknevin bog. Initially built as a horseboat, she had an engine installed during the 20's .She is reputed to have a bullet hole on the port side near the stern that she received during the Easter rising in 1916.

In the early 60's 34 B was owned by Jim Dillon who carried out the first conversion on her. David Wheeler and Tom Hodson later purchased her in a derelict state at Rathangan and they were responsible for the present conversion. Jeff Lovegrove was described as the ship's carpenter on 34 B during this period. A syndicate of German nationals along with the late Des Barry later owned her.  In the late 80's she had the dubious distinction of passing through Athlone without using the lock. On that occasion vandals cut her loose from the Apparel jetty and she went over the weir. She is currently privately owned, much loved and actively used.
