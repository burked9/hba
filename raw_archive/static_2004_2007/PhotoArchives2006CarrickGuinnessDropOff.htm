The Heritage Boat Association have had a very successful 2006 season so far, partly due to excellent sponsorship from Waterways Ireland, for both the boatman's reunion held in Banager early in July, and to the forthcoming HBA publication for 2006 ' Cool Metal, Clear Waters - a history of inland trading boats of Ireland'. Other events of 2006 include the Kilbeggan Challenge earlier in the year, and events later in the summer for Water Heritage Day.
 
In the north Shannon, a noteworthy visit from the Heritage Boat Association (HBA) occured on Saturday 5th August. The HBA were on their way north to the Erne system after a hugely successful visit to the Jamestown Heritage Festival over July 28-30. The HBA made an important drop off in Carrick on Shannon as part of the core HBA activities of making connections with most of the major towns along the Irish inland system as they travel through the waterways vicinity. The connections made are to celebrate the inland waterways trading history of many of the canal boat (barge) members of the HBA.
 
Many of the canal boats that attended Carrick are fine examples of the refurbishment of trading boats for leisure purposes and celebrations of our floating heritage. However 45M was the star of the show this weekend, she is the only barge on the inland waterway system that has been refurbished to her original condition as a working trading boat of the early 20th century.  She even has a Bolinder engine, one of only a few of its kind left in the country, a distinctive type of motor especially built for barges. The 45M belongs to David Coote and is often skippered by his daughter Suzy. 45M will be in the charge of the skipper of another barge, Gerry Burke from 68M for the Carrick Guinness drop.Gerry was accompanied by Andy Maloney, one of many boatmen who worked on the canal barges during their trading days. The symbolic cargo for delivery was Guinness barrels, made up from casks donated by Brian Quinn, Manager of Kilbeggan Whiskey Distillery in Kilbeggan Co Westmeath. As for the real Guinness, well that is a story for another day....
 
The HBA aimed for the Barrel Store jetty for 12noon, for a couple of hours until about 2 pm. Members of the public came along, read some of their history boards and met some of the current owners of these craft. Many barge skippers view themselves as only custodians of these great boats, guarding the heritage of the past to become the history of the future.  So lots of people made sure you they saw the Heritage Boats - maybe you have memories of the 41M? She was also present on Saturday as the last boat to have traded into Carrick many years ago.
